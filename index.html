Campfire

Dead Ground

The town fell months ago. Raid the ruins for supplies, dig into the earth for coal and iron, build walls before nightfall, and get one of the old cars running. The dead are slow by day and hungry at night.

Update log

v15.2 · Nov 25, 2026 — rain and windows

  • Rain is as loud as it should be for where you are: it follows the distance to the nearest surface the rain is actually landing on. Full in the open, softer and muffled under a roof, quieter still deep inside big buildings, silent underground.
  • Window sides: the inside edges of window openings are now drawn as the window frame in the building's trim colour instead of dark shadowed wall.

v15.1 · Nov 24, 2026 — walls and 3D sound

  • Fixed the real cause of buildings appearing with furniture, doors and curtains but no walls: regions loaded in the background were dropping every wall, floor and roof block. Tested across 26 region crossings with no missing buildings.
  • 3D sound: everything in the world now sounds like it is where it is. Volume falls off with distance, far sounds lose their crispness and echo more, sounds pan left and right as you turn, and things behind you are muffled. Zombies shuffle audibly as they get close, campfires crackle, doors, glass, gunfire, explosions, alarms and birds come from their real direction, and the eerie noises come from somewhere out in the dark.

v15 · Nov 24, 2026 — a better-built world, and fishing

  • Fixed buildings that showed their furniture, doors and curtains with no walls: nothing in a chunk shows until its walls and floor are built, and a chunk slot can no longer be mistaken for a different place.
  • Fixed roads in a second world following the first world's town layout.
  • Town houses are smaller and set back from the street, with real yards: pools with loungers, vegetable patches, hedges, back-yard trees and driveways with the family car.
  • Every town has a name, on green entrance signs with the population and on the map. Country roads have white edge lines. Rivers and ponds have reeds, cattails and lily pads.
  • New: fishing. Craft a rod (2 planks, cloth, scrap) or find one in hardware stores, cast from the bank, wait for the bobber to dip, reel in, and grill the fish at a fire.

v14.1 · Nov 24, 2026 — smoother

  • No more freezes when you cross into a new area: the world swaps regions one at a time over several frames, keeps each region's terrain heights cached, and skips re-packing regions you didn't change.
  • Fixed a leak that piled up copies of the passing airliner.
  • Clouds, airliners and birds now always show when you look up, at any render distance, and the fog no longer greys them out.

v14 · Nov 24, 2026 — realism

  • Thirst: a water bar. Bottled water and soda in stores; fill bottles at rivers and ponds (boil at a fire or risk getting sick); rain fills empty bottles.
  • Injuries: bites and gunshots can make you bleed (bandage it, or it gets infected), or hurt an arm (weaker swings, shaky aim) or a leg (limp, no sprinting). Splints speed healing; antibiotics cure infection.
  • Fatigue: sleep about once a day or your view blurs, stamina drains and you start blacking out.
  • Noise: gunshots carry 90 m, and explosions, breaking glass, engines, car alarms and lit windows at night all draw the dead.
  • The bat wears out and breaks (fix it with a plank and scrap). The pistol has a 12-round magazine: reload with R, and clear the odd jam.
  • Fresh food spoils overnight; cans keep. Nights are truly dark, with a moon that waxes and wanes, and your flashlight (T) runs on batteries.
  • Rain soaks you and chills you, snow slows you and leaves footprints, and fog closes in. Weeds creep onto the roads and abandoned cars rust and lose air as the days pass.
  • Car alarms, tyres that blow out on rubble and spikes (fit a spare wheel), and headlights left on that kill the battery.

v13 · Nov 24, 2026 — pockets, wrecks and the open sky

  • Your pants are your pockets: how many pockets decides how many hotbar slots you get (shorts 2 up to cargo pants 8). You start with random pants.
  • Backpacks set your case size. Find them (schools are full of them), sew one, and upgrade it to a hiking pack or a military rucksack.
  • Hitting zombies splashes blood on the windshield and the part of the car that hit them. Cars dent, scuff, crack their glass and lose bumpers as they take damage. Hit something fast enough and you smash through walls, glass and furniture.
  • Bulldozers that push through anything, excavators that dig (hold attack), dump trucks, and the rare self-driving Volta: open the map (M) and click where to go.
  • Driver view: mostly windshield, with the top of the dash and a sliver of hood.
  • Creative mode repairs and refuels any vehicle for free.
  • The game tells you, and everyone online, how you died.
  • Cities have bigger blocks with office towers, plus colleges. Kitchens have plates, bowls, cups and cutlery. Bathroom mirrors show your reflection.
  • Country roads climb hills smoothly. Clouds drift overhead, flocks of birds cross the sky, and the wind carries far-off moans, banging doors and howling.
  • Everyone is pixel-textured: skin, woven shirts, denim. Cigarettes and marijuana can be found and smoked.

v12 · Nov 24, 2026 — the wider world

  • Winding roads between towns, graded into the hills, that run straight out of a town street at full width so the join is seamless, with bridges over rivers and ponds. Off-road dirt trails.
  • Rivers, ponds and swimming. Mountain ranges with stone peaks, boulders and pebbles.
  • Hamlets, big cities (taller buildings, much more traffic), gas stations with pumps you can siphon, parks with benches, swings, slides and a fountain, malls with shops and a food court.
  • Airports: runway, terminal, control tower, hangar, parked airliners and a crashed one. Planes fly overhead now and then.
  • Light switches in every room. Craft wire, lamps and switches and wire up your own lights.
  • Zombies with different heights, builds, clothes, hair and beards. Pixel textures on every vehicle and part, all at one pixel size. New tents.
  • More realistic sound: footsteps, gunshots, engine, thunder, wind and rain.
  • Fixes: creative camera lock, road paint over holes, mobile controls hidden on computers.

v11 — cars and homes

  • Car textures, readable dashboards, car parts and radios, lootable store shelves, paintings, candles, lava lamps, Minecraft-style hotbar editing, branch thicknesses.

v10 — online

  • Multiplayer across devices, unique usernames, profile pictures, emotes and dances, blood on the windshield, creative palette for every block, vehicle and mob.

v9 — the dead

  • Ragdoll car hits, severed and halved limbs, rotting textures with exposed bone, flowers and coloured grass, road paint, curbs, signs and traffic lights, round covered manholes.

Surviving

Loot
Crates inside buildings hold ammo, food, scrap, fuel
Dig
Stone lies 4 blocks down; coal and iron below
Build
Zombies jump 2 blocks and smash wood, glass and doors — wall up 3 high in stone or metal
Cars
Need fuel cans; wrecks need a repair kit
Traps
Spikes wound and slow, bear traps pin a walker, tripwire bombs blow up a crowd (and you)
Sewers
Climb down open manholes in the roads. Dark, crawling with dead, better loot
Seasons
Spring, summer, autumn and winter turn every 3 days. Winter brings snow, short days and cold — stay indoors, by a fire, or wear a coat
Night
Sleep in a bed to skip to dawn (sets your respawn). In co-op both players must lie down
Caves
Openings in the hills lead down into dark tunnels with ore and stashes. Some houses have basements
Hunting
Deer and rabbits flee, wolves and boars fight back. Cook meat at a campfire; wear armor from crates, zombies and the workbench
People
Survivors live in the towns — they shoot zombies and trade. Bandits roam the wilds and shoot on sight
Phone / tablet
Drag left side to move, right side to look; on-screen buttons for the rest

The whole world is rebuilt from its seed, so a save only stores the seed plus what you changed, packed into a short code. The game also saves itself in this browser every 90 seconds. Split-screen co-op runs on one computer. Online play would need a game server, which this page can't host. Each run makes a new random world.
